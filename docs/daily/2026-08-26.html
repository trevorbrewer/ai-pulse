Research Papers

What FID Hides: Detecting, Ranking, and Diagnosing Deviations in Generative EvaluationarXiv

Researchers examine structural weaknesses in Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Kernel Inception Distance (KID), two of the most widely used metrics for ranking generative image models. The paper demonstrates that FID's reliance on only the first two moments of a distribution allows visually unrecognizable images, optimized purely to match reference statistics on ImageNet, to achieve low FID scores. The authors propose methods for detecting, ranking, and diagnosing distributional deviations that FID misses, arguing that a single scalar gap provides no calibrated test against sampling variation.

Recursive Experiential-Working Memory Evolution for Long-Horizon Agent HarnessesarXiv

The paper introduces Recuris, a memory architecture for AI agents tackling long-horizon tasks where growing interaction histories obscure task state and degrade skill selection. Recuris separates Working Memory, which tracks current task progress, from Experiential Memory, which stores reusable skills, grounding skill invocation in present needs rather than historical recency. The architecture is designed to address recursive self-improvement failures that arise when agents lose track of where they are within complex, multi-step tasks.

SPO++: Stream-Aligned Policy Optimization for Asynchronous Agentic RLarXiv

This paper addresses a throughput bottleneck in group-relative reinforcement learning, which requires waiting for sibling rollouts of the same prompt before updating a policy — a costly dependency when trajectories involve variable-length tool use. Building on Single-stream Policy Optimization (SPO), which replaces sibling rollouts with a persistent prompt-level value estimate, the authors introduce SPO++ and show that trajectory-level advantage centering generally does not improve training and can be removed. The work targets agentic RL settings where asynchronous, long-horizon rollouts make standard GRPO-style algorithms inefficient.

Parameterized Complexity of $L_p$-Lipschitz Constants for Input Convex Neural Networks and $L_p$-Norm Maximization over ZonotopesarXiv

This theoretical paper examines how hard it is to compute Lipschitz constants for two-layer input-convex neural networks (ICNNs), a restricted architecture that enforces output convexity via nonnegative weights. The authors show that computing the Lp-Lipschitz constant for ICNNs is equivalent to Lp-norm maximization over zonotopes, and analyze this problem through the lens of parameterized complexity. The work contributes formal hardness results relevant to certifying neural network robustness, a problem that remains computationally difficult even for shallow architectures.

Improving Cross-Problem Vehicle Routing with Locally Augmented Preferences and Representation DisentanglementarXiv

The paper addresses limitations in multi-task vehicle routing problem (VRP) solvers, which aim to handle multiple VRP variants within a single model without per-variant retraining. The authors identify two failure modes: reward-scale disparities and shrinking advantage signals during reinforcement learning training, and insufficient separation of variant-specific features in learned representations. They propose locally augmented preferences and representation disentanglement techniques to address both issues, improving cross-problem generalization.

Bellman Calibration for Marginalized Importance Weighting in Offline Reinforcement LearningarXiv

This paper tackles bias in offline reinforcement learning policy evaluation caused by residual occupancy-balance violations in marginalized importance weighting estimators. Existing minimax, primal-dual, and fitted fixed-point approaches can leave such violations due to function-class approximation or incomplete optimization, leading to inaccurate policy value estimates. The authors introduce Bellman Calibration, a post-processing correction that reduces these residual violations to improve evaluation accuracy without requiring re-training of the base estimator.

BrowserForge: Scaling Web Episode via Parallel Browser SandboxesarXiv

BrowserForge addresses the data scarcity problem for training web agents that operate from rendered pixels rather than HTML or accessibility trees. Public datasets for such agents typically contain only a few thousand trajectories from a narrow set of websites, which limits generalization. The paper proposes a system of parallel browser sandboxes to generate interaction trajectories at scale, aiming to enable training on a broader and larger corpus of web episodes.

FedV-KGQA: Multi-Hop Question Answering over Vertically Partitioned Knowledge GraphsarXiv

FedV-KGQA is a federated framework for answering multi-hop questions over knowledge graphs whose facts are distributed across organizationally siloed, vertically partitioned databases. Centralized knowledge graph QA systems cannot handle this setting because merging data across organizations violates governance and data sovereignty constraints. The framework enables multi-hop reasoning without centralizing data, addressing a gap in federated learning research that existing single-hop approaches do not cover.

Reading Is Not Using: Retrieval, Judgment, and the Design of AI Financial Research WorkflowsarXiv

Researchers have identified what they call a "retrieval-integration gap" in LLM-based financial analysis systems, where models successfully locate relevant information from financial disclosures but fail to consistently incorporate that information into their final judgments. The study is notable because most existing evaluations of AI financial analysts measure retrieval accuracy rather than whether retrieved content actually shapes downstream decisions. The researchers tested this by holding focal-firm information fixed while varying contextual firm data, allowing them to isolate whether LLMs genuinely use what they read. The findings have direct implications for the design of AI-assisted investment workflows, where overconfidence in retrieval performance may mask systematic failures in judgment.
