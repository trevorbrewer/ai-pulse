AI Capabilities & Model Evaluations

We're starting to leave the territory where you'd test an LLM by e.g. "create an svg of pelican on a bicycle"Twitter/@karpathy

Andrej Karpathy shared an experiment in which Claude Opus 5 was given the opening paragraph of The Lord of the Rings, a 1-million-token budget costing approximately $10, and a prompt to produce a Three.js rendering of the scene. The model ran for roughly two hours and produced 5,500 lines of procedural code animating polygon assets in 3D coordinates. Karpathy noted this illustrates a category of work that no human would undertake manually but that becomes trivially affordable with LLMs. He also identified a persistent weakness: Opus 5 could not efficiently audit its own output because it lacks native video perception or gameplay ability, forcing it to take slow screenshots and resulting in several errors.

Chollet's framing positions the current wave of reasoning-oriented models as a transitional measure rather than a final solution.

The Greenhouse and the Lens: Two Modes of Agentic AI WorkHacker News

Brett Horsting published an essay proposing a two-mode framework for agentic AI work: a "Greenhouse" mode in which an agent explores and generates possibilities with minimal constraints, and a "Lens" mode in which it focuses on evaluation and refinement against specific criteria. The piece argues that conflating these modes leads to poorly scoped agent tasks and inconsistent outputs, and that deliberate switching between them can improve reliability. The framework draws on observations from practical agent deployments rather than formal empirical study.
