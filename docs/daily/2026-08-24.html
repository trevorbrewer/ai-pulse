Anthropic's best AI model struggles to attract users as cheaper tools thriveSimon Willison

Anthropic's top-tier model is facing adoption headwinds even as the company's overall revenue continues to climb, according to figures cited in a Financial Times report. Annualized revenue reached $65 billion in July, up from $47 billion in May, and the company expects Q3 to be profitable by the same accounting methodology used for Q2. The tension highlighted in the story is that users are increasingly gravitating toward cheaper alternatives rather than Anthropic's most capable — and most expensive — offering.

Quoting Drew BreunigSimon Willison

Drew Breunig, writing via Simon Willison's link blog, describes a shift in how development teams are thinking about AI model selection and tooling investment. Before a model referred to as "Fable" arrived, teams felt little incentive to invest heavily in prompt engineering or context strategies, expecting new models to solve problems at similar or lower cost. Fable changed that calculus by being notably capable but expensive enough that teams began treating model selection and harness optimization as genuine engineering decisions rather than afterthoughts — with Opus and several other models remaining competitive for most coding tasks at lower cost.
