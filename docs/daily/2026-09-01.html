Research Papers

Context-Aware Interleaved Batching for WhisperXarXiv

Researchers have proposed Context-Aware Interleaved Batching as a method to address a known limitation of WhisperX, the popular accelerated speech transcription system. WhisperX gains speed through intra-audio batching but isolates audio segments, which causes it to lose the historical context needed for coherent punctuation and accurate terminology. The new approach interleaves batched segments with context from prior audio, aiming to preserve WhisperX's throughput advantages while reducing the hallucination loops and context loss that affect standard sequential Whisper inference.

Constant Individual Regret in General GamesarXiv

A new paper introduces ECHO-OFTRL, an algorithm that achieves constant individual regret in any finite N-player normal-form game under full-information feedback. Prior uncoupled no-regret dynamics retained a polylogarithmic dependence on the time horizon for individual regret; this work removes that dependence entirely. ECHO-OFTRL extends optimistic follow-the-regularized-leader with an exponential moving average cascade designed to handle high-order smoothness in the loss sequence.

SUN: Persistent Programs For Language-Grounded Control-to-Learning-to-Real PoliciesarXiv

Researchers introduce Semantically UNified (SUN) Programs, a framework designed to bridge model-based control and learned policies for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. A central problem the work addresses is that existing pipelines discard task semantics when transitioning from control to learning, leaving rewards hand-crafted and behavior drifting from what the control phase verified. SUN Programs are typed executable structures that preserve task semantics across the control-to-learning-to-real pipeline, aiming to keep learned policies grounded in the objectives specified at the control stage.

Sharp Approximation Rates for Neural Networks with Affine Latent ParameterizationsarXiv

This paper provides theoretical approximation rate guarantees for parameter-efficient neural network methods that generate a large network's weights from a low-dimensional latent representation. The framework formalizes such methods as a parameter generator mapping a latent vector in R^M to P_Φ parameter slots, where P_Φ is the full architecture size. Sharp bounds on approximation error are derived for affine latent parameterizations, offering a theoretical foundation for understanding when and how much capacity is lost by compressing network parameters into a low-dimensional space.

Auditing Anonymous AI Models: A Four-Stage Protocol for Black-Box Identity VerificationarXiv

The paper responds to what it characterizes as a wave of stealth frontier model releases during 2025–2026, in which models were launched anonymously on developer platforms under codenames, leaving users without reliable information about data-handling terms, supply-chain risk, or capability expectations. The authors propose a four-stage protocol for black-box identity verification of anonymous AI models, noting that no validated methodology previously existed for this task. The protocol is designed to go beyond practitioner checklists, which the authors say lack accuracy evidence, and to address the insufficiency of self-identification by the models themselves.

Configurable Semantic Chunking for Biomedical Information Extraction in Retrieval-Augmented GenerationarXiv

Building on BioMedRAG, which introduced retrieval-augmented generation with a learned chunk scorer for biomedical information extraction, this paper proposes a configurable semantic chunking framework to address BioMedRAG's reliance on fixed-size chunking. Fixed-size chunking can split semantic evidence across chunk boundaries, harming extraction quality. The proposed framework combines several strategies — entity-preserving windows, trigger-centered chunking, proposition-first extraction, and tiered trigger prioritization — to keep semantically coherent evidence together during retrieval.

Cross-Regional Grapevine Cold Hardiness Prediction via Learned Multimodal Latent RepresentationsarXiv

Researchers have proposed a multimodal deep learning approach to predict cold hardiness in grapevines across geographic regions, addressing a longstanding limitation of existing models that perform well only on local training data. Accurate daily cold hardiness predictions matter because freezing temperatures can damage dormant buds and reduce seasonal crop yield. The method learns latent representations from multiple data modalities to enable cross-regional generalization, a capability that prior biophysical, hybrid, and deep learning models have lacked due to the limited availability of cold hardiness datasets. By moving beyond site-specific training, the approach aims to make predictive tools more transferable to vineyards without extensive local data collection.

LLM Self-Improvement and Autonomous Learning

Aspire: Can Models Self-Evolve from Vague Goals?arXiv

Researchers introduce Aspire, a framework that asks whether LLMs can pursue self-improvement starting from underspecified objectives such as 'become a better physicist' rather than from human-specified tasks and metrics. The work argues that existing self-evolution pipelines reduce learning to a narrow optimization problem by pre-defining evaluation criteria, leaving open the harder question of goal interpretation and capability-gap identification. Aspire addresses this by having models interpret vague goals, decide what to learn, and assess their own progress without external scaffolding. The paper is notable for formalizing a more open-ended notion of machine self-improvement that more closely mirrors human learning processes.

S3Gym: Can LLMs Turn Self-Testing and Self-Judging into Self-Improvement?arXiv

S3Gym introduces a benchmark environment designed to test whether LLM-based agents can actively probe their own behavior, evaluate the resulting experience, and use that feedback to improve future decisions. Most existing agent benchmarks treat models as fixed policies, so the framework fills a gap by measuring the full self-testing, self-judging, and self-improving loop. The work is motivated by the observation that agents increasingly accumulate substantial behavioral experience during deployment yet rarely exploit it systematically. Results from the benchmark are intended to clarify under what conditions this closed-loop self-improvement is feasible for current models.

PaperGym: Rubric-Centered Evolution for Research-Plan GenerationarXiv

PaperGym proposes a reinforcement learning environment for training LLMs to generate research plans, using rubrics extracted from scientific papers as the reward signal. A key methodological concern the authors address is the risk of reward hacking via paraphrase: if the question and the evaluation criteria are drawn from the same source document, a model can score well by rephrasing rather than planning. The framework separates the question source from the rubric source to close this loophole. The work targets the capability gap in AI systems that must plan open-ended research rather than solve tasks with verifiable answers.

LLM Evaluation and Auditing

Stress-Testing Efficient Responsible-AI Evaluation: When Compute Savings Change Benchmark ConclusionsarXiv

This paper examines whether cost-cutting measures in responsible-AI benchmarking alter the substantive conclusions drawn about model behavior. The authors evaluate three dense and mixture-of-experts models on the BBQ and BBQ-V benchmarks under seven different protocol conditions, varying factors such as batching, quantization, and benchmark reduction. The finding motivates caution for practitioners who optimize evaluation pipelines for compute efficiency without checking whether rankings or safety conclusions remain stable under those changes. The study adds empirical grounding to a largely unexamined assumption in the responsible-AI evaluation literature.

BLOOM-WILT: Logit Tilting for Behaviour Elicitation in Automated LLM AuditingarXiv

BLOOM-WILT introduces a technique called logit tilting to improve the sample efficiency of automated LLM auditors, which are systems designed to surface model behaviors that manual testing rarely encounters. The core problem is that deployed models receive orders of magnitude more interactions than any pre-deployment evaluation, making post-deployment auditing both necessary and expensive. Logit tilting applies targeted adjustments to output probability distributions to increase the likelihood of eliciting specific behaviors without requiring gradient-based optimization. The method aims to make automated auditing practical enough to cover a wide range of specified behaviors at scale.

When Does Bigger Help? A Controlled Study of LLM Scale for Ontology LearningarXiv

This study systematically evaluates how model scale affects ontology learning performance by testing 13 models drawn from the Qwen3.5 and Qwen3.6 lineages as well as proprietary GPT variants, covering both dense and Mixture-of-Experts architectures. All models are evaluated using the OntoLearner retrieval-augmented generation pipeline under controlled conditions, allowing scale effects to be isolated from other architectural differences. The work addresses a gap in the literature where scaling effects on structured knowledge tasks like ontology learning have been poorly characterized compared to general language benchmarks. Findings are intended to inform practitioners on when investing in larger models yields measurable gains for this specific task.

Safety Research

DIASENTINEL: An Auditable Multi-Agent System for Guideline-Grounded Diabetes Risk ScreeningarXiv

DIASENTINEL is a fully on-premise multi-agent system designed for one-year type 2 diabetes mellitus risk screening from electronic health records, with explicit mechanisms to mitigate LLM hallucination and unsupported clinical recommendations. Running entirely on local infrastructure addresses data privacy requirements common in healthcare settings, and the system grounds its outputs in established clinical guidelines to reduce citation errors. The architecture incorporates calibrated uncertainty estimates alongside its risk assessments, providing clinicians with a signal about prediction confidence. The system targets a known failure mode of LLM-based clinical decision support tools, namely their tendency to generate plausible-sounding but unverified medical claims.

"Train classical, deploy quantum" requires rethinking generalizationarXiv

This paper argues that generative models trained on classical hardware and then transferred to quantum circuits face a fundamental generalization problem that has not been adequately addressed in the quantum machine learning literature. Quantum generative models are of interest because quantum circuits naturally sample from the distributions they encode, and those distributions are believed to be classically hard to simulate for certain circuit types. However, the authors show that standard generalization theory does not straightforwardly carry over when the deployment substrate differs from the training substrate. The work calls for new theoretical frameworks to characterize what a quantum-deployed model actually learns relative to its classical training objective.

Ontology and Knowledge Engineering

OntoAligner-Ensemble: Voting-Based Fusion across Heterogeneous Ontology Alignment TechniquesarXiv

OntoAligner-Ensemble introduces a voting-based fusion mechanism to combine outputs from ontology alignment systems that use fundamentally different underlying approaches, including lexical aligners, structural aligners, knowledge graph embedding models, and LLM-based methods. The motivation is that each approach captures complementary aspects of ontology structure, yet existing unified frameworks lack principled ways to reconcile their sometimes contradictory alignments. By treating each aligner as a voter, the ensemble can aggregate agreement signals without requiring a single methodology to dominate. The work extends the OntoAligner ecosystem and is positioned as a practical tool for practitioners who need robust alignment across heterogeneous ontologies.

Statistical Modeling and Machine Learning Methods

Implementing neural network mixed-effects models in Template Model Builder (TMB)arXiv

This paper describes an implementation of neural network mixed-effects models (NMMs) within Template Model Builder (TMB), a statistical computing framework that supports automatic differentiation. Existing NMM estimation approaches typically require manual derivation of objective functions and gradients, which forces simplifying approximations and limits model complexity. By leveraging TMB's automatic differentiation capabilities, the authors avoid these manual derivations and can fit more flexible models with fewer approximation constraints. The work is relevant to fields such as pharmacometrics and longitudinal data analysis where capturing individual-level random effects alongside complex nonlinear patterns is important.

On the Complexity of the Compatibility Problem for Succinctly Encoded Conditional DistributionsarXiv

This theoretical paper investigates the computational complexity of determining whether two conditional probability distributions, p(x|y) and p(y|x), are jointly compatible with any well-defined joint distribution p(x,y). The compatibility problem is practically relevant because probabilistic models in machine learning often output conditionals that may implicitly assume inconsistent joint distributions. The paper focuses specifically on the succinctly encoded setting, where the conditionals are represented compactly rather than as full tables, which is the regime relevant to learned models. Understanding the hardness of this problem has implications for diagnosing and designing internally consistent probabilistic models.

Industry Perspectives on LLM Development

LLM Post-Training as Brownfield Maintenance: An Industrial Perspective on Dataware EngineeringarXiv

This paper reframes industrial LLM post-training as a software brownfield maintenance problem rather than a clean-slate research exercise, arguing that practitioners typically inherit a deployed model checkpoint and must make targeted improvements without regressing existing capabilities or exceeding fixed compute budgets. The authors introduce the concept of 'dataware,' defining model behavior as governed primarily by curated post-training data mixtures that are updated through bounded patches rather than full retraining. The industrial perspective is grounded in code-generation settings as a running example, making the analysis concrete. The paper proposes dataware engineering as a discipline for systematically managing these incremental data-driven updates to deployed models.
