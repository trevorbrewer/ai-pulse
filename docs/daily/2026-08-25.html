Research Papers

How to Train a Critic Stably and EfficientlyarXiv

Researchers have published Best-Practice Critic Optimization (BPCO), a training recipe designed to stabilize critic-based reinforcement learning for large language models. The work addresses a known problem with group-based methods like GRPO, which sidestep training a critic entirely by sampling multiple responses per prompt at significant computational cost. A reliable critic could instead estimate token-level advantages from a single response, and BPCO aims to make that path tractable by diagnosing and correcting the instabilities that have historically plagued critic training.

ReWorld: An Interactive World Model with Long-Horizon MemoryarXiv

ReWorld is an interactive world model designed to simultaneously follow user actions, retain spatial memory across long sequences, and stream output in real time. The core technical challenge is that action control favors short context windows while spatial memory requires an unbounded one; ReWorld addresses this by separating the two objectives during training and bounding them differently at inference. The architecture uses mixed per-head attention windows, where most heads attend only to the recent past and a small set of global heads attends over the full history.

SWE Refactor Bench: Can Coding Agents Complete a Long-Horizon, Whole-Repository Stack Migration?arXiv

SWE Refactor Bench is a new benchmark that tests whether coding agents can perform full repository-level stack migrations rather than isolated bug fixes. Existing benchmarks measure only behavioral correctness, which the authors argue allows agents to pass tests without actually completing the migration, a gap the new benchmark is designed to close. The work is motivated by the observation that technical debt accumulated over decades makes such migrations expensive and largely manual, and that growing agent capability in narrower coding tasks has outpaced evaluation frameworks.

EG-ARSA: An Expert-Grounded Open Model for Visual Road Safety Auditing in Low-Resource SettingsarXiv

Researchers propose Expert-Grounded Distillation (EGD), a framework that transfers institutional road safety knowledge into an open AI model for visual road safety auditing, targeting low- and middle-income countries. The motivation is that such countries face high rates of road traffic injuries while lacking sufficient qualified auditors, complete crash records, and budget for large-scale field inspections. By grounding the model in expert knowledge rather than relying solely on data-driven learning, the approach attempts to compensate for the sparse labeled datasets typical in low-resource settings.

Correcting a learned physical invariant improves world-model rolloutsarXiv

Researchers tested whether a frozen DreamerV3 model trained on pendulum video implicitly learns a conserved scalar — analogous to an energy invariant — within its latent transition dynamics, without being explicitly trained to do so. A label-free search procedure successfully recovered the same energy-like invariant across independently trained conservative models, while finding no comparable invariant in non-conservative settings, suggesting the conservation is genuine rather than an artifact of the search. The study then showed that correcting rollouts using this recovered invariant improves prediction quality over time, pointing to a way of leveraging implicit physical structure in world models without modifying training.

Research Papers: Machine Learning Methods

Provably adaptive sampling with uniform and remasking discrete diffusion modelsarXiv

Researchers have analyzed sampling efficiency in discrete diffusion models, which offer parallel token generation as an alternative to autoregressive methods. The study focuses on how the choice of forward process affects the number of steps required to generate high-quality outputs, noting that existing lower bounds for the standard τ-leaping sampler scale linearly with ambient dimension d. The paper identifies conditions under which uniform and remasking forward processes allow provably adaptive sampling, potentially reducing this dimensional dependence.

ConvergeFlow: Language Flow with Provable Convergence to Token EmbeddingsarXiv

ConvergeFlow is a new continuous flow-based language model framework that addresses a structural limitation in existing continuous diffusion and flow models: their trajectories are not guaranteed to terminate at valid token embeddings, forcing reliance on cross-entropy-supervised decoders. The proposed method introduces an embedding-constrained flow formulation with provable convergence guarantees to the token embedding space. This removes the need for a separate decoder stage while maintaining performance competitive with discrete language models.

Interpretable AI with Local DistillationarXiv

This paper proposes local distillation as a method for extracting interpretable linear approximations from high-performing but opaque models such as tabular foundation models and gradient-boosted ensembles. The approach exploits the fact that a smooth regression function can be locally well approximated by a linear one, allowing practitioners to reason about individual predictions without sacrificing the predictive accuracy of the underlying model. The method is positioned as a practical path toward meeting interpretability requirements in high-stakes decision settings.

Inertial Manifold Neural Operator for Dissipative Time-Dependent Partial Differential EquationsarXiv

The Inertial Manifold Neural Operator (IMNO) is introduced for solving dissipative time-dependent partial differential equations by explicitly exploiting the low-dimensional structure that emerges in their long-time dynamics. Unlike standard architectures such as the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO), IMNO encodes an inertial manifold constraint directly into the network design, aiming to improve accuracy and efficiency on problems where dissipation drives the system toward a compact attractor. The approach targets a class of PDEs where standard neural operators may waste capacity modeling transient high-dimensional behavior.

Research Papers: AI Agents and Multi-Agent Systems

Prime Agent: A Self-Improving RLM HarnessarXiv

Prime Agent is an open-source harness designed for long-horizon evaluation and coding-agent workflows, built around the Recursive Language Model (RLM) abstraction. It provides a persistent IPython REPL for programmatic context processing and test-time computation, addressing the limitation that language models as sequential processors lack access to external information across extended tasks. The system incorporates a continual learning component, allowing the agent to accumulate experience across runs rather than resetting state between evaluations.

The Interaction Tax: When Communication Erases Diversity in Multi-Agent TeamsarXiv

This paper examines whether communication between LLM agents in multi-agent teams reliably improves outputs, finding that interaction can actually reduce diversity by pushing agents toward consensus rather than maintaining independent perspectives. The study engages with a contested literature: some prior work by Du et al. (2024) and Wang et al. (2025) reports gains from debate and mixture-of-agents synthesis, while other studies find that interaction adds cost without quality improvement under equal compute budgets. The authors frame the net cost of homogenization as an "interaction tax" and analyze conditions under which it dominates any coordination benefit.

Research Papers: AI Safety and Robustness

Robustness of Anomaly Detection Models for Industrial Control Systems under Training-Time Data ContaminationarXiv

This paper evaluates how machine-learning-based anomaly detection systems for industrial control systems (ICS) behave when their training data has been corrupted, a scenario largely unexamined in prior literature. Sources of contamination considered include compromised logs, labeling errors, manipulated historian records, and unsafe retraining pipelines — all realistic threats in operational ICS environments. The study assesses multiple offline detection pipelines to quantify how different contamination types and rates degrade detection performance.

Adapter-Based Few-Shot Continual Learning for Malicious Packet RecognitionarXiv

The paper addresses the Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning (FSCIL) setting for malware detection in network traffic, where models must recognize new malware variants from limited examples without forgetting previously learned classes. Standard continual learning approaches face catastrophic forgetting when updated on new data, while full retraining is computationally expensive in rapidly evolving threat environments. The proposed adapter-based method inserts lightweight trainable modules into a frozen backbone, allowing the model to incorporate new threat classes using only a small number of labeled malicious packet samples.

Research Papers: Human-AI Interaction and Cognitive Effects

How AI Assistance Affects Human Skill Development: A Study of Learning with Logic PuzzlesarXiv

A controlled experiment using logic puzzles examined the trade-off between short-term performance gains from AI assistance and longer-term skill development, finding that lower-cost access to AI help leads to greater reliance and reduced independent skill acquisition. Participants completed tasks before, during, and after an AI assistance period, with the cost of requesting help varied experimentally across groups. The results suggest that frictions in accessing AI tools — such as requiring explicit effort to invoke assistance — may preserve skill development even when the assistance itself is high quality.

Research Papers: AI in Healthcare and Clinical Applications

Physics-Constrained Deep Learning Model for Contactless Blood Pressure Monitoring from Triaxial BodyseismographyarXiv

Researchers present a physics-constrained deep learning model for estimating blood pressure from ballistocardiography (BCG), a contactless sensing modality that measures body vibrations caused by cardiac activity. A core challenge addressed is that BCG signals vary with body-bed interaction and individual hemodynamics, producing misaligned temporal and amplitude representations that reduce model generalizability. By incorporating physical constraints into the model architecture, the approach aims to improve robustness across subjects and measurement sessions in laboratory settings.

Predicting Multiple Clinical Outcomes Related to Functional Recovery and Social Isolation Among Older Adults After Lower-Limb Fracture or Hip ReplacementarXiv

This study applies machine learning to predict multiple simultaneous clinical outcomes — including functional recovery and social isolation — for older adults recovering at home after lower-limb fracture or hip replacement surgery. The work uses the MAISON-LLF dataset, which contains multimodal sensor and clinical assessment data from 18 older adults, and treats recovery outcomes jointly rather than in isolation. Modeling outcomes together is intended to reveal interdependencies that single-outcome studies may obscure, providing a more complete picture of post-surgical recovery trajectories.
