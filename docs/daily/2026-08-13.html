Infrastructure and Hardware

Why space is a terrible place to cool a data centerHacker News

The New Stack examines proposals for orbital AI data centers, as discussed in the context of SpaceX and Nvidia's reported interest in space-based computing infrastructure. The piece details why space is a poor environment for heat dissipation: in a vacuum, the only available cooling mechanism is thermal radiation, which is far less efficient than the liquid or air cooling used in terrestrial facilities. At current GPU power densities — Nvidia's H100 chips can draw over 700 watts each — the thermal management challenge alone would make orbital data centers impractical with existing hardware.

Research Papers

AVA-Encoder: Towards Agent-Native Video Representation LearningarXiv

Researchers introduce the Agentic Video Auto-Encoder (AVA-Encoder), a framework designed to give AI agents a structured video representation that is both faithful to film content and directly usable for agentic reasoning and manipulation. The work targets a gap between high-quality human filmmaking and what current generative video agents can produce, attributing the gap partly to the lack of suitable intermediate representations. The encoder is intended to serve as a bridge so that agents can learn cinematographic conventions from existing films.

DreamFly: Causal Memory and Receding-Horizon Diffusion Planning for Aerial Vision-Language NavigationarXiv

DreamFly is a proposed system for aerial vision-language navigation that pairs a causal memory module with receding-horizon diffusion-based planning, allowing a drone agent to integrate visual evidence over time and extend its planning horizon beyond what current vision-language-action models support. The paper identifies limited historical context and short planning horizons as the two primary obstacles to adapting existing VLA models to aerial navigation under partial observability. Receding-horizon planning means the agent continuously re-plans as new observations arrive rather than committing to a fixed sequence of actions.

AI4AI at Test-Time: Strong-to-Weak Capability Transfer via HarnessesarXiv

This paper investigates whether a stronger AI model can improve a weaker one at inference time without updating the weaker model's parameters, a departure from standard distillation approaches that require retraining. The authors study what they call strong-to-weak scaffolding, in which a builder model constructs inference-time harnesses — structured prompting or orchestration wrappers — that guide the weaker model's outputs. The work is motivated by scenarios where parameter updates to a deployed model are impractical or undesirable.

Redistribution-based Cost Inference Improves Sparse Safe Offline RLarXiv

The paper introduces the Redistribution-based Cost Inference (RCI) framework to address a practical limitation in safe offline reinforcement learning: in real settings, supervisors typically provide only a binary stop-signal at the first unsafe transition rather than the dense per-step cost annotations that most safe RL algorithms assume. RCI frames this as a temporal credit assignment problem and converts the sparse stop-feedback into dense per-step cost estimates. The approach aims to make safe offline RL viable in domains where annotating every timestep is infeasible.

Constructing Dynamic Master Logic Models as Knowledge Graphs for Complex System Diagnostics Using Retrieval-Augmented Large Language ModelsarXiv

This study presents a framework that uses retrieval-augmented large language models to automatically construct Dynamic Master Logic (DML) models — hierarchical representations linking a system's functional objectives to its structural components — from technical documentation. Traditional DML construction requires expert interpretation of documentation, which limits scalability for large or complex systems. By encoding the resulting models as knowledge graphs, the framework makes the diagnostic representations machine-queryable and easier to update.

A Framework for Designing Reward Functions: From Objectives to Features to Human-Aligned Reward FunctionsarXiv

Researchers present a formal three-step process for allowing non-experts to design reward functions that adhere to a specified preference ordering over agent trajectories. Given a natural-language task description, the process distills the task into fundamental objectives, derives measurable outcome variables, and produces a linear reward function. The work addresses a persistent challenge in reinforcement learning: reward misspecification often stems from the difficulty of translating human intent into mathematical reward signals without domain expertise.

Class Activation Mapping in Explainable Computer Vision: A Method-Centered Review of CNN, Transformer, and Foundation-Model-Era Visual ExplanationsarXiv

This review surveys the development of class activation mapping (CAM) techniques from the original 2016 formulation through their application to CNNs, vision transformers, and foundation models. CAM methods convert internal model evidence into heatmaps that highlight which image regions, channels, or tokens support a particular prediction, making them one of the most widely deployed families of visual explanation tools. The review is organized around methodological distinctions rather than chronology, offering practitioners a structured comparison of approaches across model architectures.

Beyond Trial-and-Error: Agentic Optimization for Image-to-Video AdherencearXiv

The paper proposes an agentic optimization pipeline designed to improve the reliability of black-box image-to-video (I2V) models, which currently require repetitive manual prompt and hyperparameter tuning because their outputs vary stochastically. The system treats the I2V model as a black box and uses an outer optimization agent to iteratively adjust inputs based on adherence feedback, reducing the need for brute-force trial-and-error. The work targets professional content-creation workflows where inconsistent outputs from tools like commercial I2V models create significant friction.

Large Language Model-Driven Small-Capitalization Trading: Integrating Financial News Sentiment, Macroeconomic Indicators, and Technical SignalsarXiv

This paper examines a trading strategy for small-capitalization equities that feeds LLM-extracted sentiment signals from financial news alongside macroeconomic indicators and technical signals into a portfolio construction framework. A notable methodological contribution is the decomposition of model-predicted risk into aleatoric and epistemic components, which are then incorporated directly into the covariance matrix used by the portfolio allocator rather than treated as a fixed parameter. The authors argue this uncertainty-aware construction produces more robust allocations than using fixed-lexicon sentiment scoring.

VAKRA: Evaluating Multi-Hop Reasoning Across APIs and Retrieval Under Tool-Use PoliciesarXiv

Researchers have released VAKRA, a benchmark designed to evaluate AI agents on multi-hop reasoning tasks that combine structured API calls with document retrieval, two capabilities that existing benchmarks typically assess in isolation. The benchmark comprises over 8,000 executable APIs spanning 62 domains, with tasks organized across three settings of increasing difficulty to reflect realistic enterprise deployment conditions. The work addresses a notable gap in evaluation methodology, as enterprise agents must routinely chain API interactions and knowledge retrieval steps under real-world tool-use policies. By unifying these evaluation axes, VAKRA aims to give practitioners a more complete picture of agent capability before production deployment.
