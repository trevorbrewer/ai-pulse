AI Safety and Agent Security

Show HN: Talos – An AI agent with a permission kernel between model and shellHacker News

Talos is an AI agent that inserts a permission kernel between the language model and the system shell, requiring explicit approval before the model can execute shell commands. The approach addresses a known risk in agentic workflows where models with shell access can perform unintended or destructive operations. By treating permissions as a first-class kernel-level concern rather than an application-level afterthought, Talos offers a structural constraint on what actions an agent can take without human sign-off.

AI Agent Has RootHacker News

A post on Infernal Code examines the security implications of AI agents that are granted root or administrator privileges on host systems. The author argues that running agents with elevated permissions dramatically widens the blast radius of prompt injection, runaway tool calls, or model errors. The piece urges practitioners to apply least-privilege principles to agent deployments just as they would to any other automated system process.

AI Limitations and Criticism

Your AGENTS.md file doesn't do anythingHacker News

A piece published on Pivot to AI argues that AGENTS.md files — configuration or instruction files intended to guide AI coding agents within a repository — do not reliably influence agent behavior in practice. The author contends that the files have become a form of cargo-cult practice, adopted widely despite limited evidence that agents consistently read, interpret, or act on their contents. The critique is directed at practitioners who treat such files as a meaningful control mechanism without empirical verification of their effect.
