Research Papers

Reasoning Core: Designing Broad Procedural Data for Completion-Supervised Reasoning TrainingarXiv

Researchers introduce Reasoning Core, a collection of 50 procedural generators designed to produce verifiable reasoning problems for completion-supervised fine-tuning of language models. The generators span nine domains including mathematics, logic, planning, causality, and code, and include semantic scorers, difficulty controls, and task evaluators. The work addresses a gap in the training data literature, where procedural generators have been used to produce reinforcement-learning-style rewards but have received less attention as sources of supervised fine-tuning data.

Argus: A General-Purpose Agentic Runtime for Long-Horizon ReasoningarXiv

Argus is a persistent, self-evolving agentic runtime designed for long-horizon reasoning tasks where an AI system must maintain progress over extended sequences of actions while adapting when evidence reveals failure or misspecified objectives. The system organizes work across four roles — Manager, Planner, Engineer, and Reviewer — that execute bounded missions over durable project state. A key design choice is separating stable user intent from volatile tactical decisions, allowing the runtime to pivot strategies without losing overall goal fidelity.

OctoLong: Mid-Training On Cross-Repository Code Contexts Enhances Long-Context ModelingarXiv

OctoLong is a context extension approach that uses cross-repository code contexts during mid-training to improve long-context modeling in language models. The method targets a known limitation in existing long-context corpora — that books, academic articles, and single code repositories are finite and often lack long-distance dependencies — by constructing training data that spans multiple related repositories. The work is positioned as a practical path to improving agentic and in-context learning workflows that require coherent reasoning over very long inputs.

Toward Skill-Native LLMs: Skill Entropy for Benchmarking and Training Long-Horizon ReasoningarXiv

This paper introduces the concept of skill entropy as a metric for evaluating and training large language models on cross-skill long-horizon tasks — problems that require switching between distinct reasoning skills, such as performing a mathematical derivation and then using the result to plan a schedule. The authors argue that existing benchmarks evaluate individual skills in isolation rather than the skill-switching behavior needed for realistic multi-step reasoning. The proposed framework aims to produce models that are skill-native, meaning they can fluidly coordinate different competencies within a single reasoning chain.

Teaching Nemotron Greek: Mining a Corpus, Adapting Retrieval, and Grounding Generation for Modern Greek across Specialist DomainsarXiv

Researchers present an end-to-end adaptation of NVIDIA's Nemotron retrieval stack for Modern Greek, a language currently absent from Nemotron's retrieval models and from major multilingual retrieval benchmarks. The work covers corpus mining, synthetic supervision, retrieval model training, reranking, and grounded generation across specialist domains including legal, energy, financial, and medical applications. The project highlights a gap in multilingual retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) coverage for European languages with significant domain-specific information needs.

The Loss Does Not See the Basis, but Adam DoesarXiv

This paper analyzes why gradient descent on a factored model W = UV^T converges to low-rank solutions while the Adam optimizer, starting from identical small initializations, does not exhibit the same implicit bias. The authors trace the difference to gauge symmetry — the loss's invariance under the transformation (U, V) → (UQ, VQ) for orthogonal Q — and show that low-rank implicit bias is only accessible to optimizers that are gauge-equivariant, a condition Adam fails to satisfy. The theoretical result has practical implications for understanding rank dynamics in matrix factorization and transformer weight training.

Predicting Brain Morphometry with MT-GNN: Mesh Evolution in Continuous Time with Graph-Based Metric Tensor EmbeddingsarXiv

MT-GNN is a graph neural network approach for predicting how subcortical brain structures change shape over time, using a small number of prior MRI scans as input. Rather than regressing vertex deformations directly or relying on high-dimensional latent embeddings of shape, the method predicts the surface's intrinsic geometry — specifically its metric tensor — in continuous time. The approach is intended to support clinical prognosis and the enrichment of clinical trials by identifying patients likely to show measurable structural progression.

OPD-V: Visual On-Policy Self-Distillation with Modality BalancearXiv

Researchers have proposed OPD-V, a method designed to address modality imbalance in on-policy self-distillation for multimodal large language models (MLLMs). On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) has become a standard post-training technique for improving visual reasoning, but existing approaches tend to let textual information dominate generation, causing the model to underutilize visual inputs. OPD-V introduces a modality-balancing mechanism during self-distillation to ensure visual signals contribute more equitably alongside text. The work is notable because modality imbalance is a structural challenge common to MLLM reasoning pipelines that prior OPSD methods have not explicitly tackled.
