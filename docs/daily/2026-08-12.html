Research & Best Practices

There are no lossless transformations of natural-language textSimon Willison

Simon Willison highlights a post by Sophie Alpert outlining an internal policy on acceptable AI writing use for engineers, centered on the principle that any text an LLM produces must be fully owned and verified by the person publishing it. Alpert's core argument, which Willison endorses, is that there are no lossless transformations of natural-language text — meaning any AI-assisted rephrasing changes meaning in ways that may not be immediately apparent. The policy requires that engineers stand behind every idea and every sentence in their documentation, regardless of whether AI was used to assist in drafting.

Research Papers: Multimodal and GUI Models

Test-Time Self-Evolving GUI Visual Grounding via Reflection-Guided On-Policy Self-DistillationarXiv

Researchers propose a Test-Time Self-Evolving framework that allows GUI visual grounding models to adapt their parameters after deployment without requiring additional labeled data. Existing approaches freeze model weights post-training, which limits performance on unseen interfaces, while test-time reinforcement learning methods lack the ability to reflect on failed explorations. The proposed framework addresses this by incorporating reflection-guided on-policy self-distillation, enabling the model to learn from its own mistakes during inference.

MultiModal Code-Switching: Interleaving Visual Objects into Language for Explicit Object-Level AlignmentarXiv

A paper identifies a limitation in current multimodal large language models (MLLMs), which predominantly use image-text pairs for alignment pretraining and map global image representations to textual descriptions, resulting in referential ambiguity when multiple objects are present. The authors propose a multimodal code-switching approach that interleaves individual visual object tokens directly into the language sequence, enabling explicit object-level rather than image-level alignment. The method targets scenarios where models must distinguish and correctly associate several visual entities with their corresponding textual references.

A Quantum Roadmap for Softmax Attention: Exact Born-Rule Analogs for Softmax Attention on the Probability SimplexarXiv

A paper establishes that in settings where transformer attention inputs and outputs are constrained to the probability simplex, softmax attention admits an exact quantum realization on a component-by-component basis. The quantum analog uses Hadamard-test statistics on block-encoded projectors to compute attention scores, directly mirroring the Born rule of quantum mechanics. The authors frame this as a roadmap for future quantum implementations of attention mechanisms in the specific subclass of problems where outputs must sum to one.
