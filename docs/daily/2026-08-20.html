DFlash 2: Keep Drafting ParallelHacker News

Inco AI has published details on DFlash 2, a speculative decoding method designed to accelerate large language model inference by running draft generation steps in parallel. Speculative decoding works by using a smaller model to propose token sequences that a larger model then verifies in fewer forward passes, reducing latency. The DFlash 2 approach extends this by allowing multiple draft sequences to be evaluated concurrently, aiming to improve throughput on hardware setups where parallelism is available.

Unsloth Dynamic 3.0 GGUFsHacker News

Unsloth released Dynamic 3.0 GGUFs, an updated quantization format for large language models designed to preserve model quality at lower bit depths by applying non-uniform quantization across layers. The approach selectively assigns higher precision to layers deemed more sensitive to quantization error, rather than applying a single bit-width uniformly. This is notable for practitioners running inference locally, as it offers a way to reduce model size while minimizing accuracy degradation compared to standard GGUF quantization.

Research Papers

SPADE: Self-Play in Adaptive Synthetic Executable EnvironmentsarXiv

Researchers introduced SPADE, a self-play reinforcement learning framework in which a single large language model acts as both environment generator and agent, continuously creating new goals as the learner's capability improves. The framework addresses a known limitation of existing training setups, where hand-curated or statically synthesized task pools fix the difficulty distribution and cannot adapt to a more capable model. By generating executable synthetic environments with verifiable outcomes, SPADE enables open-ended skill acquisition without relying on frozen verifiers or human-designed curricula.

ADEPT: Accelerating Dexterity via Pre-Training and Post-Training using Reinforcement LearningarXiv

ADEPT is a large-scale reinforcement learning framework for teaching dexterous manipulation to high-degree-of-freedom robot hands, using raw visuo-tactile perception rather than engineered state representations. The method first pretrains a policy on a generic object reposing task to build general motor skills, then applies post-training to adapt that policy to specific downstream tasks. The sim-to-real transfer approach targets long-horizon manipulation tasks, a domain where most prior RL methods have required privileged simulation state inputs.

Beyond Teacher Likelihood: Group-Calibrated On-Policy Distillation for Long-Context ReasoningarXiv

This paper identifies a failure mode in on-policy distillation (OPD) for long-context reasoning tasks, where token-level guidance from a teacher model can favor locally fluent responses that nonetheless violate global constraints or miss evidence spread across a long input. The authors propose group-calibrated on-policy distillation, which combines dense teacher signals with response-level verification to correct for this bias. The method is motivated by the observation that task-specific verifiers and teacher likelihoods capture complementary aspects of response quality that neither approach captures alone.

Finetuning Strategies for Querying Sounds by Vocal ImitationarXiv

This technical report describes the winning submission to the AES AIMLA 2025 Challenge on sound-effect retrieval via vocal imitation, a task where users hum or vocalize to locate matching audio. The team tested two fine-tuning strategies: contrastive learning with a frozen CED encoder, and joint contrastive-triplet learning using semi-hard negatives with a MobileNetV3 encoder. The updated report includes additional experimental details added after the competition for reproducibility.

Lévy Attention: Single-Pass Predictive Uncertainty for Continuous-Time AttentionarXiv

Researchers propose Lévy Attention, an attention mechanism for irregularly-sampled time series that produces calibrated uncertainty estimates at arbitrary continuous timestamps within a single forward pass, with no additional computational cost at inference time. The method replaces standard attention with a stochastic formulation based on Lévy processes, which yields closed-form uncertainty alongside each prediction. This is notable for applications such as medical monitoring or sensor data, where time series observations arrive at irregular intervals and trustworthiness of model outputs varies depending on how far a query timestamp falls from observed data.

Learned, Then Lost: A Measured Single-Example Counterfactual in Pre-trainingarXiv

Researchers conducted a controlled experiment to directly measure, rather than estimate, the causal effect of a single training example on a finished language model. The study trained 32 GPT-2 models at 124 million parameters from scratch on OpenWebText across four conditions and eight random seeds, running the costly counterfactual comparison 24 times. The experimental design isolates the contribution of one data point by differing two full pre-training runs by only a single row in a single batch, an approach rarely attempted due to its computational expense. Results were observed at step 200 of a 9,536-step training run, providing early-stage evidence of how individual examples shape model behavior.
