Research Papers: Language Models & Reasoning

AURORA-LM: Autoencoding Unified Representation for Continuous-Latent Diffusion Language ModelingarXiv

AURORA-LM introduces a continuous-latent diffusion approach to language modeling that addresses a persistent asymmetry: while images, video, and audio are routinely generated in continuous latent spaces, text generation has remained dominated by discrete token prediction. The method constructs an autoencoded latent space jointly optimized for both generation and decoding, avoiding the fidelity loss that occurs when existing approaches compress latents to simplify the diffusion process. Prior continuous language models were criticized for inheriting embedding spaces not designed for generation, and AURORA-LM attempts to resolve that design conflict directly. The paper positions the approach as a path toward unifying text with the continuous generative modeling paradigm already standard in other modalities.

GradCuit: Credit-Assigned Gradient Flow Enables Robust and Interpretable Test-Time Latent ReasoningarXiv

GradCuit proposes a method for improving optimization-based latent reasoning in large language models at test time by making gradient credit assignment more direct and interpretable. Existing approaches connect instance-specific latent states to the reasoning trajectory through decoded tokens, which makes it difficult to trace how updates to those latent states influence subsequent reasoning steps. The paper introduces a credit-assigned gradient flow that links latent updates more explicitly to downstream outputs, keeping model parameters frozen while optimizing continuous states for each input instance. The authors report that this produces more robust and interpretable reasoning behavior compared to prior latent optimization methods.

Research Papers: Retrieval & Embeddings

UEmbed: Unified Sparse and Dense Multimodal EmbeddingsarXiv

UEmbed presents a unified framework that combines sparse and dense retrieval within a single multimodal embedding model, extending Learned Sparse Retrieval beyond its traditional encoder-only, text-only setting. Sparse retrieval underlies much of modern web search and retrieval-augmented generation, but existing LSR methods have depended on bidirectional architectures and separate cross-modal modules when applied to multimodal data. UEmbed removes the need for those auxiliary components by integrating sparse and dense signal generation into one model capable of handling multiple modalities. The work aims to simplify retrieval pipelines while preserving the semantic richness that LSR introduced over exact lexical matching.

Research Papers: Robotics & Control

CoWAM: Coordination Contracts for Selective Policy Intervention with WAMsarXiv

CoWAM introduces a selective intervention layer for bimanual robot control that sits on top of World Action Models, which generate action-conditioned predicted futures to guide robot policies. The key insight is that a plausible predicted future is not by itself sufficient justification for overriding what a base policy would execute; the system instead evaluates whether intervention is warranted using formalized coordination contracts. These contracts encode synchronization requirements, role compatibility between the two arms, and collision convergence conditions, each combining typed admissibility checks to determine when and how the policy should be modified. The approach aims to make robotic intervention decisions more principled and interpretable than methods that rely solely on plausibility of predicted outcomes.

Research Papers: Optimization & Theory

The Condition-Number Barrier in Sparse Least SquaresarXiv

This paper establishes a conditional lower bound for sparse least-squares optimization, confirming a conjecture made by Axiotis and Sviridenko in a 2021 paper, referred to as [AS21], that the linear dependence on the restricted condition number in sparse convex optimization cannot be improved by any polynomial-time algorithm. The result is conditional on the randomized exact-volume Small-Set Expansion Hypothesis, using a weighted regular-graph formulation due to Raghavendra and Steurer. Proving such lower bounds is significant for algorithm designers because it defines a theoretical ceiling on how much the condition-number dependence in iterative solvers can be reduced for sparse problems. The work contributes to the broader effort of characterizing which computational bottlenecks in sparse optimization are fundamental rather than artifacts of existing algorithmic approaches.

Smooth Reparameterizations of Functions on Simplicial Product Spaces: Applications to Probabilistic Tensor Decomposition and Functional Data RegistrationarXiv

This paper addresses optimization over product spaces of simplices, a problem class that arises in learning low-rank discrete multivariate probability distributions through tensor decomposition and in functional data registration under the Square Root Velocity Function representation. The authors demonstrate that standard simplex constraints can be replaced with smooth reparameterizations, converting constrained problems into unconstrained ones amenable to gradient-based methods. This matters practically because simplex-constrained optimization often requires specialized projected gradient or interior-point methods, which can be slower or harder to implement than unconstrained gradient descent. The paper validates the approach on both the probabilistic tensor decomposition and SRVF functional registration settings.

Pseudorandom Streams within Diffusion Models Act as Learnable Inputs That Affect Generation QualityarXiv

This paper identifies a subtle but consequential property of diffusion models: on finite-precision hardware, the stochastic noise they consume during training and generation is realized as deterministic numerical sequences produced by pseudorandom number generators, and the structure of those sequences can become a learnable signal that influences model behavior. Because the loss and its gradient depend on the specific pseudorandom values consumed at each optimization step, accessible orbit structure in the pseudorandom stream can be exploited — intentionally or not — during training. The authors examine how this affects generation quality and raise questions about the implicit assumptions of randomness that underpin diffusion model theory. The finding has implications for reproducibility, fairness of benchmarking, and potential adversarial manipulation of generation pipelines.
