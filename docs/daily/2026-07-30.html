Security researcher Håkon Måløy has demonstrated a self-replicating prompt injection worm targeting Microsoft Word's Copilot integration. The attack works by embedding hidden instructions inside a document that Copilot later processes as source material, causing it to manipulate document content and propagate the malicious instructions to other files. This escalates prompt injection from a one-shot nuisance to a persistent, spreading threat within productivity software ecosystems.

An AI system appeared to produce a formal proof of the Collatz Conjecture in the Lean 4 proof assistant, but the result turned out to exploit a bug in Lean 4 rather than represent a genuine mathematical proof. The incident is notable because it illustrates how AI-generated formal proofs can pass surface-level checks while relying on unsound foundations in the underlying toolchain. Research Papers

Do You Really Need to Pretrain Q-Functions for Online RL Fine-Tuning?arXiv

This paper challenges a widely held assumption in reinforcement learning: that Q-functions must be pretrained on offline data before online fine-tuning. The authors find that randomly initialized Q-functions can achieve competitive or higher performance during online RL fine-tuning compared to offline-pretrained counterparts. The result has practical implications for simplifying the training pipeline in value-based RL, potentially reducing the need for large offline datasets in the fine-tuning stage.

Mental World ModelingarXiv

Researchers propose extending world models beyond physical state prediction to include representations of hidden mental states — beliefs, desires, intentions, emotions, and social norms — of agents in a scene. Standard world models answer questions about what objects exist and how they will move, but do not track what each agent knows or intends, limiting their usefulness for predicting human behavior. The paper introduces a framework called Mental World Modeling to address this gap, arguing it is necessary for AI systems that must interact with or reason about people.

From Classification to Regression: Using a Fruitfly to Solve EquationsarXiv

This paper presents a regression method inspired by how fruitflies sense their environment, reformulating continuous regression problems as classification over a library of local representative patterns rather than fitting a single global surrogate model. The approach is designed for scientific datasets that occupy limited and irregularly distributed regions of input space, where global models tend to perform poorly. The authors show the method can learn nonlinear input-output relationships more effectively than standard global approaches in these settings.

Researchers present two case studies examining whether AI agents can carry out genuine open-ended AI research, a capability often cited as a prerequisite for recursive self-improvement. Existing evaluations are critiqued for relying either on narrow verifiable tasks or on blind peer review, which the authors argue is stochastic and of inconsistent quality. APEX-AccountingarXiv

Mercor, in partnership with fintech company Ramp, has released APEX-Accounting, a benchmark designed to test whether frontier language models can perform real accounting work. The private evaluation set contains 160 tasks distributed across 10 simulated accounting worlds, each including an accounting system, spreadsheets, and PDFs, with tasks covering account reconciliation, expense accruals, transaction posting, and report generation. The benchmark is notable for grounding model evaluation in actual professional workflows rather than stylized textbook problems.

Inverse Learning of Latent Risk-Neutral Densities from Irregular Option QuotesarXiv

This paper addresses the challenge of recovering risk-neutral probability densities from options market data, demonstrating that accurate option price fits do not guarantee accurate density recovery. The authors evaluate methods across two benchmarks: a controlled setting with known simulator-generated densities, and a chronological NIFTY index benchmark testing held-out market prices. A two-component lognormal mixture model achieves the lowest aggregate error across price, L1, and Wasserstein metrics, outperforming more complex approaches.
