Research Papers

Don't Drop the BATON: Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation via Agentic Subtask Exploration and Transition-aware MemoryarXiv

Researchers propose BATON, a framework for enabling robots to complete long-horizon manipulation tasks — sequences of many contact-rich steps — by pairing a frozen vision-language-action model with an LLM agent that handles high-level planning and error recovery. The key insight is that standard VLA models, while competent at individual skills, accumulate errors across multi-stage tasks because each subtask silently constrains the conditions needed for the next. BATON addresses this through agentic subtask exploration and a transition-aware memory module that tracks how the state of the environment evolves between steps. The approach keeps the low-level VLA policy unchanged, which means improvements generalize without retraining the base model.

Q-based Variational Inverse Reinforcement LearningarXiv

The paper introduces Q-based Variational IRL (QVIRL), a Bayesian method for inferring reward functions from expert behavior without requiring explicit hand-specification of human preferences. Standard inverse reinforcement learning methods often struggle with scalability and uncertainty quantification; QVIRL addresses this by framing reward inference as a variational Bayesian problem grounded in Q-value estimation. The work is positioned as a contribution to AI safety and alignment research, where the ability to reliably recover human preferences from observed behavior is considered a core technical challenge. The authors report experimental results demonstrating that QVIRL produces well-calibrated reward estimates across a set of standard benchmark environments.

Spectral Gaps of Hit-and-Run and Coordinate Hit-and-RunarXiv

This paper establishes new spectral gap bounds for Hit-and-Run and Coordinate Hit-and-Run, two Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithms widely used for sampling from convex bodies. The authors prove that for any convex body in n-dimensional space containing a unit ball, the spectral gap of Hit-and-Run is at least Omega(1 / (n^2 * C_PI)), where C_PI is the Poincaré constant of the uniform distribution over the body. This implies that the algorithm converges to within chi-squared divergence epsilon of the uniform distribution in O(n^2 * C_PI * log(1/epsilon)) steps. The result is relevant to practitioners in Bayesian computation, optimization, and machine learning who rely on these samplers for high-dimensional inference tasks.

AutoSR: Automatic Symbolic Regression by Searching Research StatesarXiv

Researchers have introduced AutoSR, a fully automated symbolic regression system that frames equation discovery as a persistent scientific investigation rather than a search over isolated expressions. The motivation is that finite, noisy datasets can produce numerically competitive equations with very different behaviors outside the observed data range, making raw numerical fit and syntactic complexity insufficient criteria for selecting a correct model. AutoSR addresses this by maintaining and searching "research states" — structured records of ongoing investigations — so that candidate expressions are evaluated in a broader scientific context rather than on a single data snapshot. The approach targets a known weakness in standard symbolic regression benchmarks, where methods are judged on in-distribution accuracy alone.
